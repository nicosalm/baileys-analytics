Getting the most out of your Baileys Analytics subscription.

Every dashboard in Baileys Analytics is a reflection of your OwnerRez account. Spend twenty minutes cleaning it up, and the numbers that come out the other side get sharper overnight.

Baileys does not invent data. It surfaces what OwnerRez exposes, and when a field is missing upstream, the dashboards show that honestly rather than guessing. The trade-off is that a few blank fields in OwnerRez can make whole sections of the app look empty or lopsided. This post is a checklist of the five things to fix, in order of impact.

Before you start: Baileys picks up most OwnerRez changes automatically via webhooks, usually within minutes. For a full refresh that reprocesses the last four years of bookings, open Settings → Integrations and click Re-sync All Data. Every re-sync re-categorizes your historical bookings against the current OwnerRez data, so a rename today fixes the whole history, not just new reservations.

1. Tag every property

Tags are the most powerful filter in the app. Every dashboard, every chart, every metric can be sliced by tag. Selecting a single tag like lakefront narrows the whole app to just that subset. Selecting two or more widens the view to include anything matching any of them. That is how you pull up every lakefront and hot-tub home in one click, without going property by property.

Tags come directly from OwnerRez. Add them on each property there, and Baileys picks them up on the next sync. Use the dimensions you actually manage with: location, bedroom count, pet policy, amenities, whatever matches how you think about the portfolio.

If you are tagging a large portfolio for the first time, one warning: tag names are matched as-is, so Lake Access and lake-access count as two different tags. Pick a convention and stick to it. Five tags you actually use beats twenty that are nearly identical.

2. Name your surcharges so Baileys can categorize them

Baileys breaks your P&L into four surcharge buckets: Cleaning & Linens, Pet Fees, Credit Card Fees, and Other Surcharges. The routing is a simple keyword rule against the charge description you set in OwnerRez:

  • Descriptions containing clean or linen route to Cleaning & Linens
  • Descriptions containing pet route to Pet Fees
  • Descriptions containing credit card route to Credit Card Fees
  • Everything else lands in Other Surcharges

If your charge composition donut has a large Other Surcharges wedge, that is almost always a naming problem upstream. A template called Housekeeping Fee lands in Other because the word "clean" does not appear in it. Rename it to Cleaning Fee and the whole history moves to the right bucket on the next sync. Same pattern for pet fees called Animal Deposit and credit card surcharges called Processing Fee. The rule reads the charge description, not the charge type, so rename the template, not the type.

3. Clean up your custom booking source names

Baileys automatically normalizes the major channels. Airbnb, VRBO, Booking.com, Expedia, Google Vacation Rentals, Hopper, HomeToGo, and TripAdvisor all get detected regardless of capitalization or minor spelling differences. Direct-style sources (anything containing direct, owner, website, or repeat, plus any source that is a raw domain like doorcountystay.com) are all rolled up as Direct. Anything else falls into a bucket called Other.

To find out whether this is affecting you, open the Properties dashboard and look at the Booking Sources chart. If you see a meaningful Other slice, rename those sources in OwnerRez so they contain a keyword Baileys recognizes. Renaming only affects the display label; it does not touch channel sync, historical reports, or anything else inside OwnerRez.

One more thing worth tidying: if you have three variants of the same channel (say Manual, Phone, and Walk-in) that all really mean "direct," consolidating them prevents your platform breakdown from fragmenting into slivers.

4. Set a Private Code for each property

OwnerRez has a field called Private Code on every property, usually a short two or three letter abbreviation. Baileys uses that code on space-constrained views where the full property name would wrap or clip: stacked bars, small tables, property tiles on the performance matrix, and the compact charts on the properties dashboard.

If the field is blank, Baileys falls back to the full property name and truncates it, which often looks strange (Harbor Light Cot...). Two or three letters is enough. Set it once in OwnerRez, re-sync, and every compact chart in the app becomes immediately readable.

5. Fill in the Baileys side

Two things to set up inside Baileys itself:

  • Operating expenses. Add your recurring monthly costs under Settings → Expenses. Categories are cleaning, management, utilities, maintenance, insurance, and other. Mortgage, property tax, software, and accounting fees all go under other. Save a cost as a template on a property and it applies to every future month automatically, so you enter each number once instead of every month. Without these, the P&L only reflects booking-level costs and your net margin looks higher than reality.
  • Goals. Set portfolio-wide targets under Settings → Goals. Seven metrics are supported, from revenue and occupancy through to average stay length and repeat guest rate, each on a monthly, quarterly, or yearly cadence. Progress shows up in the "Your Goals" panel on the overview dashboard, and an active monthly revenue goal also draws a reference line on the revenue composition chart.

Taxes are not something you configure in Baileys. If OwnerRez is collecting and recording taxes on your bookings, Baileys reads them straight off the charges and splits gross revenue from tax automatically. If your tax numbers look wrong, the fix is in OwnerRez tax rules, not on our side.
